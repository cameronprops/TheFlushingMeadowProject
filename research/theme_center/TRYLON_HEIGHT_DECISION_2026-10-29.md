# Trylon Height: Authoritative Reconstruction Decision

**Decision date:** 2026-10-29
**Status:** Adopted project modeling standard (source-backed, pending master mesh QA)
**Authoritative height:** 610 ft = 185.928 m

## Primary sources
- *The Structural Engineer*, volume 16, issue 11 (1938), "Theme Structures of New York World's Fair": a three-sided steel tower of 610 ft, with 480 ft of diagonally braced framework plus 130 ft of upper plate construction. https://www.istructe.org/journal/volumes/volume-16-%28published-in-1938%29/issue-11/theme-structures-of-new-york-world-s-fair/
- American Bridge Company advertisement in *The American Foreign Service Journal*, September 1938, p. 563: 610 ft. https://afsa.org/sites/default/files/fsj-1938-09-september_0.pdf

## Independent secondary corroboration
- Smithsonian National Museum of American History: 610 ft. https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_1129265
- Cooper Hewitt: 610 ft. https://www.cooperhewitt.org/2017/03/13/fair-wear/

## Modeling contract
- Set the 1939 as-built Trylon master to height 610 ft (185.928 m).
- Structural transition occurs 480 ft above the article's assumed base datum; investigate foundation/grade datum before precision placement.
- Keep other reported 675-ft and 700-ft figures in the evidence ledger as conflicting historical claims, not model options. Reopen only if convincing as-built primary documentation emerges.
- In CAD, enforce envelope height and the 480-ft/130-ft structural division; leave other unconfirmed dimensions provisional.
- Do not silently change Perisphere diameter or the shared coordinate datum from this decision.

**This decision does not itself certify the extant CAD mesh or all structural details.**
