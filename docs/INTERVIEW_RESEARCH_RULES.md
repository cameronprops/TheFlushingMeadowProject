# Recorded interviews and oral histories

An oral interview is attributed testimony about experiences, designs and recollections. It is **not** direct as-built evidence unless corroborated by drawings, period photos or operating documents.

Record speaker, interviewer, interview date, years being recalled, hosting archive and stable catalog link. Mark whether full transcript, excerpt, media recording, or just catalog entry has been reviewed. Never present inaccessible audio as acquired; leave timecodes unset if not confirmed.

Each claim is linked to the interview and an optional venue, with verification status, source section and potential contradictory period documents. Do not fabricate a narrator voice or reproduce a real speaker's voice for the game without appropriate permission.

Use interviews as both historical research and leads for possible interactive experiences, but keep visitor quotes, copyrighted dialogues and creative-story direction subject to rights review and the project's creative controls.

Known holdings: NYPL's 12 sound recordings are **not confirmed to be oral interviews**. Queens Museum's 2023 recording event requires actual accession checks.
